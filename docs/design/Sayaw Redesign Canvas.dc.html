TURN 1 · SAYAW OPERATOR REDESIGN

A night you can run without being a DJ

Eleven artboards. The central change: the two audio decks stop being the interface. The screen is now a handover lane — what the floor is dancing to, what is coming, and one plain sentence saying what happens and when. Deck A and B survive only as lane colour (lavender ■ on the floor / mint ■ coming in), never as a word the operator has to learn. Amber ■ is always the voice.

WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY
1

Six unlabelled icons became two words. Sources, set shape, offline prep, Snowball and Jack & Jill all moved into Adjust (1g); fullscreen became Stage view. Nothing on the main screen is guessable-only.

2

The plain sentence is the biggest thing on screen after the clock. The old announcer strip stated a label and a timing fragment; this states the event, the delay and the words the room will hear. It keeps its box when there is nothing to say, so nothing below it moves.

3

“Deck A / Deck B” is gone. Two decks are now one handover lane: what is going out on the left, what is coming in on the right, in the two mandated hues and lightnesses. The whole 44dp band is draggable, as it already was — but it is now labelled in words, not with the letters A and B.

4

Two equal actions, one named after the music. Pause is the state, “Start Bachata now” is the act — 72dp tall, 12dp apart, in the same coordinates in every layout. The old bar had six identical squares (cue / play / load × 2) plus FADE; cue and load are now automatic and demoted to Adjust.

5

Three panes, three plain nouns. Library → Music, Queue → Set list, Decks → Now. Same three destinations as the rail at wider widths, in the same order.

WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY
1

Wider windows earn one extra word, not extra controls: Prepare appears beside Adjust because a desktop is where the night gets set up an hour early.

2

The centre column is byte-identical to compact. Same cards, same order, same sizes; the wide window adds panes on either side rather than rearranging what a hand already knows.

3

The set list states relationships, not chips. “on the floor now” and “coming in · says …” replace a left-border colour the newcomer had to decode, and the two rows carry the same two lane hues as the handover lane.

4

Action bar pinned to the bottom of the centre pane — the same offset from the bottom edge as compact, so muscle memory survives a mid-set window drag in either direction.

Rail order and destinations are unchanged from the existing SayawPane enum, so the width-only breakpoint logic and its tests stay valid.

WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY
1

Not a bigger main screen — a different question answered. Stage view answers “what is playing, how long, what next” from three metres away: dance name at 112px, clock at 180px, sentence at 40px. Panes, search and set list are all dropped.

2

The amber sentence survives into stage view because it is the one thing a caller reads out loud. Its box is fixed-height; when nothing is queued it says “Nothing after this song” in the same slot.

3

A visible way out. The old mode was exitable by F11 or Esc — invisible on a tablet. A labelled fourth button sits beside the transport, which keeps the three transport controls in their existing left-to-right order.

Borderless and chrome-free as today: no app bar, no title bar, near-black ground so nothing glows on a stage monitor.

1

1

The drag says what will happen, in words. An amber line names the row in hand, and the drop target states the consequence — “plays 11th, after Obsesión” — instead of leaving the operator to count rows.

WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY
1

One screen, not two. Dance type and announcement were separate concerns in separate places (row edit vs. the “Announce with” dialog). They are one decision — the dance decides the words — so they are one screen, in the order a person thinks in.

2

Options are named by outcome. “Nothing / Whistle / duck level 0.3” became “what the room hears”, and the ducking explanation moved into the option that causes it: music dips underneath the voice.

3

Tag once, apply to the dance. Tagging 34 rows by hand is why volunteers give up. Default on, reversible per row — and it states how many rows it touches.

4

A read-back sentence in the same amber as the main screen, so the wording learned here is the wording read there. Preview is explicitly headphones only — the old dialog refused a preview entirely rather than risk a whistle over a full floor; naming the output makes it safe to offer.

WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY
1

Split by time, not by feature. Everything you do at six o'clock is a checklist; everything you do at eleven is a sheet over the set. The old app bar mixed both into one row of icons.

2

The pre-flight report became the screen. Event Mode used to be a modal you had to know to open, whose report vanished on close. Here its findings are the checklist, each with the one action that resolves it.

3

Adjust opens with the two things anyone actually changes mid-night — blend length and announcement mode — and states the safety rule at the top: changes land at the next transition.

4

Genuinely-expert controls sit at the bottom, in plain words with the jargon as the value. “Blend shape · Equal-power — no dip in the middle”. A working DJ finds all six curves behind it; nobody else has to meet the word.

WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY
1

Failures are counted and visible before they are audible. A silent announcement used to look identical to a working one; a missing file only announced itself as a greyed row someone had to notice. Now: a red count in the ribbon, and the sentence slot goes red.

2

The failure occupies the exact slot the good news occupied — same position, same height, same 26px sentence. Nothing under a reaching hand moves, which was a hard constraint. The two causes are labelled SONG and VOICE so a compound failure doesn’t read as one vague problem.

3

Three ways out, and a default that fires by itself. The volunteer who freezes still gets music: doing nothing performs the safe option at zero. A dialog would have blocked the transport instead.

4

The primary action is visibly disabled and says why — “nothing playable is cued”. The old bar disabled buttons by dropping opacity to 38% with no reason given.

Scope is stated once, not per row: “5 more songs live on that drive” replaces the forty-toast failure mode the existing network banner was built to avoid.

WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY

“Soundboard” and “cue” are gone. These are “your sounds”, and the thing that decides whether one is usable over vocals is stated as an outcome — music dips under it — not as a duck level.

The keyboard shortcut is printed on every row, because a working DJ wants keys 1–9 and a first-timer needs to know the number under the button means something.

Where the sound goes is stated once, plainly. The old picker refused a preview rather than risk a whistle over a full floor; naming the destination is the smaller, kinder fix.
